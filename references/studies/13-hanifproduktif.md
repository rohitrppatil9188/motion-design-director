## Typography and hierarchy

The main family is a heavy sans with moderate width and tight spacing. Weight, width, split masks and per-character delays are separate expressive controls. The most readable moments are allowed to resolve into a normal line before the next transformation.

## Composition and spatial structure

Single-word cards use large quiet fields. Baselines, targets and underlines make the transformation legible. The circular letter study changes the layout family while keeping the film typographic.

## Colour, gradients and material

Black/cream with vivid orange and acid lime form four clearly separated states. The source largely uses solid fields; colourful edge echoes on a snap card are a transient accent rather than a full-film gradient style.

## Motion mechanics and transitions

Native-frame windows show the iris, anticipated exit, staggered character assembly, horizontal slice and deliberate abrupt palette cuts. The particle ending should maintain a stable point population and converge before switching to clean solid text.

## Pace and density

Most behaviour demonstrations occupy about one second, with a longer roughly two-second shape/orbit passage and two-second final assembly/hold. It is a catalogue, so the label itself is part of the effect.

## Transferable construction

When teaching motion through words, make each verb's behaviour distinct and include a recognisable settled state. Demonstration clarity can be more impressive than continuous complexity.
